# How a buy-side analyst monitors London offices today

Research notes gathered on 2026-10-23 by a research sub-agent using web search; figures carry their source and date inline, and items marked unverified were not confirmed against a primary source.

## London office analyst: how the job is done today

This is research only. I wrote no files, though WebFetch cached some broker PDFs in the session's tool-results folder on its own.

### 1. Routine and cadence
- **Daily: news and agent emails.** A Blackstone London real estate associate starts around 7:30 with "the FT and React News" and works "80% in Excel and 20% in PowerPoint" ([eFC](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2022/07/blackstone-real-estate-day-in-life)). React News, now renamed Green Street News, sends morning and afternoon alerts and runs a Deal Tracker ([React](https://reactnews.com/?p=24), [GSN](https://greenstreetnews.com/?p=24)). EG, CoStar News and Bisnow round out the feed; that analysts read these is my inference.
- **Weekly: screening and tracking.** Job ads ask for "screening and analysing new investment opportunities", IC materials and "quarterly reporting" ([Michael Page](https://michaelpage.co.uk/job-detail/pe-real-estate-investment-analyst/ref/jn-092026-7097213)), and to "identify and track UK and European targets" ([Gold Group](https://www.goldgroup.co.uk/job/investment-analyst-3)). A weekly pipeline meeting is unverified.
- **Monthly:** CBRE Central London Figures. The April 2026 issue gave take-up 0.6m sq ft, availability 20.1m, under offer 3.9m and vacancy 6.8% ([CBRE](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/8bf540c6-08db-481d-be33-fee447f043e8-30436712/Central_London_Office_Figures_.pdf)). Savills also publishes monthly City and West End data.
- **Quarterly:** reports from Knight Frank (London Offices Spotlight), BNP Paribas RE, Cushman & Wakefield (MarketBeat), Savills, Avison Young and Colliers. LSH's series is called "UK Investment Transactions" (UKIT), not "investment transactions". IPF Consensus Forecasts are also quarterly.
- **Twice-yearly or annual:**
  - The Bayes CRE Lending Report comes out twice a year ([Bayes](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/990531/CRE-Presentation-YE-2025.pdf)).
  - The Deloitte Crane Survey has been annual since April 2026. It was twice-yearly before, and covers schemes of 10,000 sq ft or more ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/deloitte-london-office-crane-survey-april-2026.html)).
- **Databases:** the job ads I could read name only Excel and Argus, so the rest of the stack is inferred:
  - CoStar.
  - MSCI, which has owned RCA since 2021 and runs the former IPD indices ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210913005804/en/MSCI-Completes-Acquisition-of-Real-Capital-Analytics)).
  - EG Radius, which EG says replaced EGi ([EG](https://eg.co.uk/?p=1090785)).
  - PMA and broker portals: unverified.

### 2. Deliverables
- **Quarterly market commentary to an investment committee:** the City of London Corporation's recurring "Appendix D – Market Commentary" is ten numbered points ([CoL](https://democracy.cityoflondon.gov.uk/documents/s183034/05%20-%20Appendix%20D%20-%20Market%20Commentary.pdf)). It pairs two sources:
  - Savills leasing data: take-up against the 10-year average, average prime, Grade A and Grade B rents, supply and vacancy.
  - JLL investment data: volumes, largest deal, buyer origin (Asia-Pacific 44% in 2022) and prime yields by lot size (West End 4.00% for £40–125m lots, 4.25% above that).
- **Comparables:** leasing comps record building, sq ft, occupier and rent per sq ft, often "blended" or "confidential". Investment comps record price, £ per sq ft, net initial yield and buyer ([KF Q2 2026](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/site-assets/research/report-pdfs/london-office-spotlight/spotlight-q2-2026.pdf)). RICS ranks completed deals (Category A) above databases and broker data (Category B), and says headline rents must be adjusted for rent-free periods and other incentives ([RICS](https://rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/comparable_evidence_in_real_estate_valuation.pdf)).
- **Other outputs:** screening notes, trackers and the IC memo market section show up only in job ads. Internal formats and any weekly update are unverified.

### 3. KPIs and definitions
- **Knight Frank's rules** ([KF](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/1903/documents/en/london-offices-spotlight-q2-2024-11338.pdf)):
  - Availability is space free now plus unlet space finishing within 6 months.
  - Take-up includes pre-lets and purchases for occupation.
  - Demand counts named requirements over 10,000 sq ft.
  - The pipeline counts schemes over 20,000 sq ft.
  - Investment counts office deals of £1m or more, including site purchases.
  - "Second-hand Grade A" only means previously occupied with air-conditioning.
- **Other brokers' rules:**
  - CBRE availability adds space being marketed that will be ready within 12 months. Its pre-let figure means "let or under offer": 47% of the 11.1m sq ft under construction at end-2025 ([CBRE](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/d2d710e2-b30e-40c5-8112-ac09d0165b3a-709626602/Central_London_Office_Figures_.pdf)).
  - BNP's take-up counts deals over 1,000 sq ft ([BNP](https://www.realestate.bnpparibas.co.uk/sites/default/files/2025-11/BNPPRECentralLondonOfficeMarketUpdateQ32025.pdf)).
  - Cushman & Wakefield's annual London Moves counts deals over 5,000 sq ft ([MPA](https://www.mpamag.com/uk/mortgage-types/commercial/central-london-office-demand-rises-as-expansions-hit-six-year-high/575426)).
- **"Prime" means different things:**
  - Savills reports average achieved prime and Grade A rents: City £105.26 vs £74.34, West End £166.61 vs £99.58 at end-2025 ([Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk/research_articles/229130/386949-0/central-london-office-market-watch-q4-2025)).
  - BNP splits "prime" (City £87.50) from "premium" (£140).
  - Knight Frank quotes headline prime with rent-free: City Core £102.50 with 24 months, West End Core £185 with 21 months.
  - Over a 10-year term, City Core nets to about £82 effective. The 10-year term is my assumption; Knight Frank doesn't state the term.

- **Geography:** CBRE uses five markets, Knight Frank uses 20 submarkets including Stratford and White City, and Avison Young has a "Tech Belt".

### 4. Where the time goes
- **Reconciling series:** much of the work is turning conflicting series into one house view, and no broker publishes a crosswalk between them (my inference).
- **Extraction:** the figures sit in designed PDFs and charts. Knight Frank's Q3 2025 report was over the 10MB fetch limit, and the CBRE, Knight Frank and BNP PDFs had to be rendered as images to read them (my own experience here).
- **Spreadsheets:** analysts "gather data points from different reports… populate a spreadsheet cell by cell", according to a vendor, the CEO of Fundrise and RealAI ([O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/podcast/generative-ai-in-the-real-world-ai-for-real-estate-with-ben-miller/)). An AREF/Yardi study of 24 UK managers flags data fragmentation as a key problem ([AREF](https://www.aref.org.uk/resource/post-event-write-up-yardi-aref-report-and-webinar.html)).
- **Comps gaps and lags:** RICS says published deal data lags the actual deal by months. EG says Radius leaves out renewals and NDA deals.
- **News overload:** plausible, but I found no figures (unverified).

### 5. How opportunities are spotted
- **Off-market deals:** 76% of West End volume in July 2022 ([Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk/research_articles/229130/331762-0)), and 69% of City deals in the first half of 2019 ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/london/capital-markets/there-is-nothing-to-buy-in-london-right-now-99591)).
- **Debt maturities:**
  - About £33bn of UK CRE debt matures in 2026, and 13% of loans have interest cover below 1x. These are Bayes figures from a secondary report ([Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/specialist-lending/commercial-finance/2026/09/17/commercial-property-doesnt-need-a-boom-to-create-opportunity-breeden/)).
  - Purchases made in 2017–18, typically on roughly 5-year loans, are worth screening. Cheung Kei bought 20 Canada Square for £410m. Its £265m loan went unpaid, receivers came in in June 2023, and the guide price was £250m ([Bisnow](https://bisnow.com/london/news/capital-markets/london-office-bought-for-410m-goes-into-receivership-119384)). 5 Churchill Place followed the same path ([Bisnow](https://bisnow.com/london/news/capital-markets/sign-of-the-times-a-hong-kong-based-owner-is-walking-away-from-a-270m-london-office-building-119975)).
- **Fund wind-downs:** about £3.5bn of UK-exposed funds were being liquidated in 2024, including Credit Suisse REF International (82% offices) ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/london/capital-markets/everything-must-go-the-great-35b-fund-selloff-125635)).
- **Lease expiries:** CoStar projects 32m sq ft of expiries in 2025–26, up 23%. Only the summary is public ([CoStar](https://costar.com/article/1992818792/london-office-lease-expiries-to-surge-in-2025-26-but-will-demand-follow)).
- **Planning and pipeline:** the GLA's Planning London Datahub has live borough feeds and an API ([GLA](https://www.london.gov.uk/what-we-do/planning/digital-planning-0/planning-london-datahub)). Deloitte counted 4.8m sq ft of starts in 2025, 66% of them refurbishments, and flags a possible supply gap in 2027–30.
- **ESG stranding:**
  - 38% of London office stock is rated below EPC C ([KF](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2024/5/insight-6-retrofit-or-repurpose)).
  - The June 2026 interim government response dropped the planned 2027 EPC C step. It requires EPC B from 2031, only for buildings over 1,000 m² and only where cost-effective. It is not yet law ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/09/nondomestic-mees-uk-government-confirms-epc-b-target-for-larger-commercial-buildings-from-2031)).
- **Pricing:**
  - Q2 2026 deals ranged from £208 per sq ft (55 Old Broad Street, City) to £3,278 per sq ft (14 St George Street, Mayfair).
  - Derwent, GPE and Helical trade about 40–45% below their net asset value ([QuotedData](https://quoteddata.com/2026/05/why-the-market-may-be-too-bearish-on-london-offices/)).
  - Replacement-cost benchmarks: unverified.
- **Hong Kong and Asian investors:**
  - **Trophy wave:** in 2017–18, Hong Kong buyers bought trophy towers such as the Walkie Talkie (about £1.3bn) and the Leadenhall Building (£1.15bn) ([IREI](https://irei.com/news/hong-kong-investor-acquires-landmark-office-london-1-3b)). A Knight Frank investment agent said at the time that trophies are "a very good medium for investing a large amount of money quickly" ([City AM](https://www.cityam.com/287575-ubs-london-hq-sold-gbp1bn-asian-investment-london-real/)).
  - **Retreat:** Asia's share of Central London office investment fell from 44% in 2022 (JLL) to 11% in 2025 (CBRE); the two figures come from different sources. Hong Kong owners are now selling ([Bisnow](https://bisnow.com/london/news/capital-markets/hong-kong-investor-eyes-900m-double-london-exit-124884)).
  - **Currency hedging:** CC Land, the Leadenhall buyer, took an £800m sterling credit facility secured on HKD and USD deposits ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/180878/cc-land-lines-up-800m-rcf-as-currency-hedge)). Whether hedging costs or earns money depends on the gap between interest rates ([Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk/blog/article/362478/commercial-property/do-currency-fluctuations-really-move-real-estate-investment-decisions-.aspx)). BNP's Larry Young said in early 2025 that rate volatility "is causing problems for any investors that need to hedge sterling" ([Bisnow](https://bisnow.com/london/news/office/london-office-deals-fell-off-a-cliff-last-year-can-they-bounce-back-in-2025-127915)). The current cost of hedging HKD to GBP is unverified.
- **Nan Fung itself** develops as well as invests. Its London arm, Endurance Land, is building 99 City Road (EC1, 475,000 sq ft, due 2030) ([Multiplex](https://www.multiplex.global/news/multiplex-appointed-as-main-contractor-for-landmark-commercial-development-99-city-road/)). That makes pre-let demand and the 2027–30 supply gap directly relevant to the target analyst.
